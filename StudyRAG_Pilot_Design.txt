STUDYRAG — PILOT DEPLOYMENT PLAN
Offline-Capable, Voice-Driven AI Teaching at Scale  |  1,000+ Users  |  20-Week Pilot
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. PRIMARY OBJECTIVES & STRUCTURE
StudyRAG is an AI tutoring platform built for environments where reliable internet is
a luxury. The pilot validates whether offline-capable, voice-driven instruction produces
measurable gains in comprehension, engagement, and retention for students excluded from
mainstream EdTech. Four phases structure the 20-week run:

  Phase 1 (Wks 1–4)   Finalize partner agreements, build localized curriculum caches,
                       run train-the-trainer workshops for educators and NGO field staff.
  Phase 2 (Wks 5–8)   Full rollout to 1,200 enrolled students. Usage telemetry collected
                       continuously and auto-synced whenever a device connects.
  Phase 3 (Wks 9–16)  Bi-weekly data reviews, OTA content updates, accessibility sprints,
                       and mid-pilot surveys at Weeks 10 and 14.
  Phase 4 (Wks 17–20) Summative assessments, satisfaction surveys, partner debriefs,
                       and a full impact report delivered within 30 days of close.

2. DURATION
The pilot runs for 20 weeks (5 months), aligned with a standard academic semester so
participation integrates into existing coursework. This length captures habit formation
and learning outcomes beyond the novelty phase that shorter pilots routinely miss.

3. PARTICIPATING USER GROUPS
We enroll 1,200 students (20% buffer) to hold an active base of at least 1,000.

  60% — Low-resource / rural students: Households with intermittent or unaffordable
  internet. Offline caching removes the connectivity dependency entirely. Recruited
  through NGO partners already trusted in these communities.

  20% — Students with Dyslexia & ADHD: StudyRAG's voice-first interface, adjustable
  pacing, short-form concept chunking, and WCAG 2.1 AA-compliant UI reduce cognitive
  and reading load. Identified through Disability Services offices.

  20% — General cohort (control): Standard-resource students with reliable connectivity,
  providing a comparative baseline for engagement and academic-performance metrics.

4. POTENTIAL PARTNERSHIPS
  • Universities & School Districts  — Distribute software via institutional channels,
    integrate participation into course credit, share anonymized academic baselines.
  • Educational Equity NGOs          — Community recruitment, localized onboarding
    workshops, device distribution, and qualitative field feedback collection.
  • Telecom Providers                — Exploring zero-rating for sync and cache traffic
    so low-income students incur no data charges during progress uploads.
  • Disability Services Offices      — Co-design accessibility rubrics, screen
    neurodivergent participants, interpret assistive-tech usage patterns.

5. PARTNER RESPONSIBILITIES
  StudyRAG            99.9% uptime SLA, data privacy compliance (FERPA/GDPR), bi-weekly
                      analytics reports, UX iteration, and 24-hour critical support SLA.
  Institutions        Enroll students, link participation to credit structures, provide
                      anonymized prior-semester academic baselines.
  NGO Partners        Recruit and onboard the low-resource cohort, distribute devices
                      where needed, gather on-the-ground qualitative feedback.
  Disability Offices  Approve all disability-segment research instruments, participate in
                      data interpretation sessions, author an accessibility findings memo.

6. LONG-TERM OUTCOMES & PERSISTENCE PATHWAYS
  • LMS Integration: AI tutoring embeds natively into partner LMS platforms (Moodle,
    Canvas, Blackboard) by Month 6 post-pilot, eliminating platform-switching friction.
  • Curriculum Intelligence: Concept-struggle analytics fed back to institutions as
    ranked curriculum-gap reports, designed to become part of annual review cycles
    independent of continued StudyRAG use.
  • Open Offline Infrastructure: The sync protocol is published under an open license
    post-pilot so other developers and institutions can apply the same offline-first
    model to any educational tool.
  • Policy Pathway: NGO and Disability Services partners co-author a public policy
    brief on EdTech connectivity and accessibility standards, delivered within 60 days
    of the pilot's close, drawing directly on disaggregated outcome data.
